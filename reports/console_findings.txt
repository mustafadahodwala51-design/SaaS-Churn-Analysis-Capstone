SECTION 2  |  FIRST LOOK AT THE DATA
============================================================================
What is one row?
  customers_raw.csv          one row = one CUSTOMER (the master list)
  customer_monthly_raw.csv   one row = one CUSTOMER in one MONTH (activity)

Columns in the customer table and what they mean:
  customer_id          Unique code for the customer (primary key)
  signup_date          Date the customer first started paying
  plan_tier            Which plan they bought (Starter / Growth / Business / Enterprise)
  industry             Industry the customer's company works in
  country              Country of the customer
  region               Wider geographic region
  company_size         Employee band of the customer (1-50, 51-200, 201-1000, 1000+)
  seats                Number of user seats bought
  billing_cycle        Monthly or Annual billing
  acquisition_channel  How the customer was first won
  discount_pct         Percentage discount off the list price
  mrr_at_signup        Monthly Recurring Revenue on day one
  status               Active or Churned at the end of the period
  churn_date           Date the customer cancelled (blank if still active)
  churn_reason         Why they cancelled (blank if still active)
  tenure_months        How many months the customer stayed
  nps_score            Net Promoter Score 0-10 given by the customer
  last_login_date      Last time anyone logged into the product
  seats_current        Seats held at the end of the period

Columns in the monthly activity table:
  customer_id          Links back to the customer table
  month_start          First day of the month this row describes
  month_number         Calendar month number (1-12)
  year                 Calendar year
  tenure_month         How many months the customer had been subscribed
  mrr                  Revenue this customer generated that month
  seats                Seats held that month
  active_users         Seats that were actually used that month
  adoption_rate        Share of seats actually used (0-1, or a percent as text)
  support_tickets      Support tickets raised that month
  nps_score            NPS given that month
  is_active            1 = the customer was active that month

SECTION 3  |  DATA QUALITY AUDIT
============================================================================
Before trusting any number we check the file for the problems that are
always present in a real data export. Findings are listed as we go.

3.1  Duplicate records
     customers_raw.csv          : 23 fully identical rows
     customer_monthly_raw.csv   : 31 fully identical rows
     Cause: the export job ran twice and appended the same rows.
     Treatment: drop_duplicates() -> removes 54 rows.

3.2  Inconsistent text values
     plan_tier       8 distinct value(s): [' business  ', ' enterprise  ', ' growth  ', ' starter  ', 'Business', 'Enterprise', 'Growth', 'Starter']
     billing_cycle   3 distinct value(s): ['Annual', 'Monthly', 'monthly']
     region          6 distinct value(s): ['Asia Pacific', 'EMEA ', 'Europe', 'Latin America', 'North America', 'north america']
     status          2 distinct value(s): ['Active', 'Churned']
     company_size    4 distinct value(s): ['1-50', '1000+', '201-1000', '51-200']
     Cause: three different systems write to this field.
     Treatment: strip spaces, then standardise capitalisation.
     plan_tier needs cleaning on 16 row(s) (stray spaces and lower case)

3.3  Numeric columns that contain text
     mrr_at_signup : 14 row(s) are not numbers (currency symbols, e.g. '$1,674.39')
     seats         : 7 row(s) are not numbers (the text 'n/a')
     Treatment: strip '$' and ',' then convert with pd.to_numeric().

3.4  Inconsistent date formats
     churn_date   : 27 row(s) use DD/MM/YYYY, the rest use YYYY-MM-DD
     signup_date  : all rows use YYYY-MM-DD
     last_login_date : all rows use YYYY-MM-DD
     Treatment: parse both formats with dayfirst=True, then store as datetime.

3.5  Missing values
     customers.seats                    7 missing ( 0.55%)
     customers.churn_date             834 missing (65.51%)
     customers.churn_reason           875 missing (68.74%)
     customers.nps_score              190 missing (14.93%)
     customers.last_login_date         97 missing ( 7.62%)

     Note: churn_reason is legitimately blank for every customer who is
           still active - that is missing BY DESIGN, not a defect.

3.6  Impossible or suspicious values
     mrr_at_signup : 3 row(s) are negative (an unapplied credit note, not a real subscription)
     seats         : 2 row(s) above 10,000 seats (double data entry - see the plan/size mismatch)
     Treatment: negative MRR set to missing; impossible seat counts set to
               missing so they cannot distort averages.

3.7  adoption_rate stored two different ways
     140 row(s) are stored as a percentage such as '45.3'
     13706 row(s) are stored as a fraction such as 0.453
     Treatment: any value above 1 is divided by 100.

3.8  Business-rule checks (does the data make sense?)
     Active customers that also have a churn_date : 0  (expected 0)
     Churned customers with no churn_date         : 0  (expected 0)

SECTION 5  |  FEATURE ENGINEERING
============================================================================
The raw columns do not answer the business questions, so we build the
columns that do. Each new column is explained below.

5.1  churn_flag = 1 if the customer cancelled, 0 if still active
       430 churned / 1,250 total = 34.4%
5.2  signup_cohort = the month the customer joined (used for cohorts)
5.3  arpu = average revenue per user (MRR / seats); arr = annualised MRR x12
5.4  engagement_band built from average seat adoption over the customer's life
5.5  final_adoption / final_tickets / final_nps / final_mrr
       = the customer's average over their LAST 3 ACTIVE months.
5.6  lifetime_value = mrr_at_signup x tenure_months (simple LTV proxy)
5.7  Joined the customer attributes onto the monthly table and added
       month and arr_run_rate (MRR x 12) for convenience.

5.8  Final column list

       customer_id, signup_date, plan_tier, industry, country, region, company_size, seats, billing_cycle, acquisition_channel, discount_pct, mrr_at_signup, status, churn_date, churn_reason, tenure_months, nps_score, last_login_date, seats_current, tenure_months_calc, churn_flag, signup_cohort, seat_estimate, is_seat_data_missing, arpu, arr, avg_adoption, engagement_band, final_adoption, final_tickets, final_nps, final_mrr, lifetime_value

KEY QUESTION 1  |  What is the state of the business right now?
============================================================================
  Total customers on the books            : 1,250
  Churned during 2023-2024                : 430 (34.4%)
  Still active in December 2024           : 820
  MRR in December 2024                    : $1,051,252
  ARR run-rate in December 2024           : $12,615,022
  Average revenue per active customer     : $1,282
  MRR lost to churn over the period       : $304,928
  Change in MRR since the Oct 2024 peak   : -0.1%
  MRR growth, last 6 months                   : +0.8% per month

  WHAT HAPPENED
  The customer base grew explosively through 2023, with MRR peaking at
  $1,052,669 in Oct 2024. Growth then stopped: average monthly
  growth fell from +51.9% to +0.8%, and active customers
  are down 3% from their Sep 2024 peak of 846.

  WHY IT MATTERS
  Revenue is now flat only because new logos almost exactly replace the ones lost:
  over the last 6 months the business added 140 new customers and lost 143 existing ones, a net -3 logos,
  while MRR grew +0.8% a month. There is no growth left to absorb a
  bad month: churn compounds on a base that is no longer expanding.

KEY QUESTION 4  |  What signals are associated with a customer leaving?
============================================================================
  We compare the final 3 months of churned customers with those still active.

  Signal                                 Churned      Active    Difference
  Seat adoption                           33.6%       38.5%        -4.9%
  Support tickets / month                   1.34        1.22         +0.12
  NPS score                                 6.85        7.07         -0.22
  Average discount %                       10.5%        9.0%          1.5pp

  WHAT HAPPENED
  Customers who churned were using a smaller share of their seats, raised
  slightly more support tickets, gave a lower NPS, and had been given a
  larger discount at signup - than customers who stayed.

  WHY IT MATTERS
  All four are recorded BEFORE the cancellation, so they can be used to
  flag accounts while there is still time to save them. Note this is an
  association, not proof of cause: low adoption may be a symptom of a
  customer who was already planning to leave.

KEY QUESTION 5  |  Is revenue retained from existing customers?
============================================================================
  Comparing December 2023 with December 2024 for the same customers:
    MRR 12 months ago (that cohort)      : $836,792
    MRR now from the customers who stayed: $756,792
    Net Revenue Retention (NRR)          : 90.4%
    of which expansion from seat growth   : +6.8 pp
    of which churn from cancellations    : -16.4 pp

  WHAT HAPPENED
  NRR of 90% means the existing customer base shrank: the company
  gave back more revenue through churn (-16.4 pp) than it
  gained from expansion (+6.8 pp).

  WHY IT MATTERS
  NRR is the single number that tells a subscription business whether it
  is getting healthier or just buying more customers to stand still.

KEY QUESTION 6  |  How much revenue is currently at risk?
============================================================================
  We build a simple, explainable risk score out of the signals that Section 8
  Question 4 identified. It is a rule-based score, not a black-box model, so
  a customer-success manager can see exactly why an account is flagged.

  Risk score = weighted sum of six signals, each rescaled to 0-1 so that
  no single measurement dominates because of its units. The SIGN of the
  weight carries the direction: a negative weight means a high value is
  protective, a positive weight means a high value is a warning sign.

    final_adoption       weight -2.0   low seat use pushes the score UP
    final_tickets        weight +1.0   more support tickets pushes the score UP
    final_nps            weight -1.2   a low NPS pushes the score UP
    final_mrr            weight +1.0   bigger accounts rank higher - more MRR is at stake
    tenure_months        weight -0.8   a short tenure pushes the score UP
    discount_pct         weight +0.6   a heavy signup discount pushes the score UP

  risk_band  customers  total_mrr  avg_score  avg_adoption  avg_nps  avg_tickets  share_of_active_mrr
   Low risk        274 266638.403     -2.142         0.662    8.320        0.681               25.545
Medium risk        273 268719.360     -0.931         0.337    7.100        1.131               25.744
  High risk        273 508458.473      0.057         0.154    5.777        1.855               48.711

  MRR sitting with HIGH-RISK active customers: $508,458 (49% of active MRR)
  These 273 accounts use 15% of their seats on average, give NPS 5.8 and raise 1.9 tickets a month.

  WHY IT MATTERS
  This is the number a retention campaign would be measured against.
  Improving adoption in this group alone protects $508,458 of
  recurring revenue - and the accounts are already identified, so the
  work can start immediately rather than waiting for a predictive model.

============================================================================
KEY QUESTION 7  |  What should the business do?
============================================================================
  The recommendations below follow directly from the evidence above.

  1. Fix the first 90 days. 38% of all cancellations happen before a customer has been
     subscribed for 3 months, and months 0-3 are the worst window of the whole
     curve (17 points of the customer base). The cancellation rate is 5.0%
     in month one. Move onboarding, activation and the first support contact
     into a structured 90-day programme instead of reactive support.

  2. Then handle the first renewal. The cancellation rate dips to about 2%
     mid-tenure and then climbs back to 3.9% around months 12-18 as the first annual
     contracts come up for decision. Start that conversation 90 days before
     the anniversary, not when the cancellation notice arrives.

  3. Stop losing Monthly accounts faster than Annual ones.
     Monthly billing churns at 37% versus 29% on Annual billing. Offer an
     annual-billing incentive at signup to close the gap.

  4. Review discounting. Churned customers were given 1.5 percentage points more discount on average
     than customers who stayed. Discounts buy contracts that do not last.
     Tie discount approval to company size and to the plan the account can
     realistically grow into.

  5. Re-balance acquisition spend away from Events.
     It has the highest churn (43.4%) and lifetime value ranked 6 of 6 ($9,699),
     yet it still produces logos. Organic keeps customers far better at 24.1%.
     Shift budget, and add a 90-day retention check before renewing
     acquisition budget.

  6. Run a targeted save programme on the high-risk accounts identified above
     rather than a company-wide discount campaign, which would cost more
     than the churn it prevents.
